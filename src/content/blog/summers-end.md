Every day the sun sets earlier, and the nights are a bit more brisk. The summer is lazily ceding ground to the autumn, and soon I will be back to my usual semester routine of research, writing, and grading. Unfortunately, dear reader, this means that we must part until at least December, if not June.

I began this summer in a very different place. I had hair down to my shoulders, I was in a relationship, and I wasn't even sure if I would keep the blog going. I did end up continuing the blog, my relationship ended, and my hair is now sitting at a tight twelve millimeters in length, if the measurements on my electric razor's attachments are accurate.

In the process of navigating these changes, I have done my best to write honestly about my own thoughts and feelings as they pass. I do my best, but there is a limit to how much I can let anyone in. There is, of course, the psychological impossibility of giving another person direct access to my own inner experience. There is also the difficulty of the medium of language. There is only so much that I can express in just a few thousand words.

But there is also the further difficulty of balancing honesty with tact, analysis with scrutiny, and communication with gossip. I can only tell as much as I am willing to share, and I can only go so far as my understanding of the truth remains self-sure. I can share with you reality as I understand it, but only so far as I have understanding.

I write not in spite of this gap between us but because of it. I want to be known, to be understood. I want you to see into my slice of reality and find something valuable. I omit and obfuscate not to obscure the truth but to protect it, so that I only go so far as to write the things that I know are true, or, at the very least, the things I strongly suspect are true and my accompanying reasons for thinking so.

I find myself often coming back to a poem from Emily Dickinson:

> Tell all the Truth but tell it slant --\
> Success in Circuit lies\
> Too bright for our infirm Delight\
> The Truth's superb surprise
> 		
> As Lightning to the Children eased\
> With explanation kind\
> The Truth must dazzle gradually\
> Or every man be blind --

As the semester begins I will have to shrug off the slant truth of my summer writing and return to the upright pursuit of truth that is analytic philosophy. I have already, in the past week, started to get back into my research and have again felt the warm blush of fresh metaphysical inquiry blooming as I read.

But I will miss the stylistic variety and contentual freedom that this format affords me. When writing for an audience of philosophers, vagueness is entirely prohibited. One must state precisely what one means before analysis can even begin. I cannot let my stream of consciousness flow onto the page unattenuated, I cannot let the contradictions and uncertainties of my mind sit unresolved on the page.

Philosophy is a useful exercise in sorting out conceptual confusion, and pursuing it for this sake is both rewarding and enlightening. It has its limits, however, and I cannot pursue every area of interest with it. So I write here. I give you, dear reader, the slanted truth as I understand it, with as little distortion as I can respectably manage.

Thank you again to those who have been reading along this summer, and for all of you who have provided such kind words of encouragement as I vivisect my life on the internet.
